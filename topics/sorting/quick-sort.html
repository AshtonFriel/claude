Quick Sort

best O(n log n)average O(n log n)worst O(n²)space O(log n)

Quick sort picks a pivot (here: the last element) and partitions the range so everything smaller sits to the pivot's left and everything larger to its right. That drops the pivot into its final position, and the two sides are sorted recursively. On average the pivot splits the range roughly in half — O(n log n) — but a consistently terrible pivot (e.g. sorted input with a last-element pivot) degrades it to O(n²).

Where you'll meet it: the workhorse behind many standard-library sorts (usually as introsort — quick sort with safeguards). In-place, cache-friendly, and typically the fastest comparison sort in practice.

⚠ Common mistakes
  • Recursing on [lo..p] instead of [lo..p−1] — the pivot is final, re-including it causes infinite recursion on duplicates.
  • Forgetting the final swap that places the pivot at the boundary — the partition silently does nothing.
  • Always picking the first/last element as pivot in production code, inviting the O(n²) sorted-input worst case (use random or median-of-three).
🎯 Interview questions that use this
  • "Find the k-th largest element" — quickselect is one partition call recursed on a single side, O(n) average.
  • "Sort colors / Dutch national flag" — a 3-way partition variant.
  • "Why does your library sort switch to heapsort sometimes?" (introsort's O(n²) safeguard).
